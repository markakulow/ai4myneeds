Find a Job

Make your job search more focused, not more exhausting.

Use AI to explore possibilities, search more intelligently and evaluate opportunities while keeping the decisions grounded in your real experience and priorities.

✓

AI can help you search and think. It can't decide fit for you. Use it to uncover options and questions, then verify current job and company information before acting on it.

How this works

Start with where you're stuck.

You don't need to overhaul your entire job search. Pick one problem below and use AI4MyNeeds to make that part easier.

Job search help

What would you like help with?

Each guide gives you a practical way to use AI without pretending AI is the job board, recruiter or hiring manager.

🎯

Figure out what roles fit me

Discover job titles and career directions that connect to your actual experience, strengths and preferences.

Try this: Give your AI assistant a summary of your work history, strengths and the conditions a job needs to fit your life. Ask it for role families and titles worth researching, including less-obvious options. Have it explain why each might fit and what gaps you should investigate rather than assuming you're qualified.

Example: “I’m available for 40 hours per week for first- or second-shift roles. I can’t work nights or weekends. Hybrid or remote would work best because I’m a single mother with two children. I’m looking for full-time roles with health insurance and benefits such as a 401(k). My experience includes [add your experience]. What types of jobs and job titles should I research?”

🔎

Search smarter

Turn your background into better titles, keywords and searches for job boards and company career sites.

Try this: Provide several target roles plus your experience. Ask AI for related titles, alternate terminology and search phrases. Then use a search engine such as Google to search across the wider web, not only one job board. Try combinations of the role, location, schedule, industry and words such as careers or jobs.

Example: “I have customer service and scheduling experience and need a weekday remote role. Give me 10 related job titles and Google searches I can use to find employer career pages, niche job boards and opportunities outside the major job platforms.”

🧩

Is this job actually a good fit?

Compare a posting with your real background and separate strong matches, transferable skills and genuine gaps.

Try this: Give AI the full job description and your resume. Ask for three sections: Strong Matches, Transferable/Underrepresented Experience and Genuine Gaps. Tell it not to invent qualifications or treat every preferred requirement as mandatory.

Example: “Compare this Customer Success Manager posting with my resume. Show me where I clearly match, where my experience may transfer even if my title was different, and any real gaps. Don’t rewrite my experience or assume skills I haven’t shown.”

🏢

Research a company

Know what to investigate before you apply, interview or decide whether an employer interests you.

Try this: Use both AI and web search. Ask AI to create a research checklist, then use Google or another search engine to verify current information from the employer, recent news and other credible sources. AI tools with live web search can help gather this information too, but always look at the underlying sources.

Example searches: “Acme Company careers,” “Acme Company layoffs 2026,” “Acme Company customer reviews,” or “Acme Company Customer Support Director.” Then ask AI to help you organize what you found into useful interview or application questions.

📋

Organize my job search

Create a simple system for applications, networking, follow-ups and the work you want to do each week.

Try this: Tell AI how much time you realistically have each week and the activities you're currently doing. Ask it to help create a manageable weekly routine plus a simple application tracker.

Example: “I have 90 minutes each weekday for my job search. Help me divide that time between finding roles, applying, researching companies, networking and follow-ups. Create a simple tracker with company, role, link, date applied, contact, status, next action and follow-up date.”

Search beyond the job boards

Use search engines and AI together.

LinkedIn and large job boards are useful, but they aren't the whole internet. Search engines can uncover employer career pages, staffing firms, associations, local organizations and niche job boards you may never think to visit directly. AI can help you create smarter searches, discover alternate job titles and make sense of what you find.

A simple pattern: Ask AI what to search for → search the live web → open the real sources → bring useful findings back to AI for comparison or organization.

Useful job-search resources

AI works better alongside the right sources.

Use AI for discovery, comparison, preparation and organization. Use current job boards, employer career pages and professional networks to verify openings and make real connections.

🌐

Job platforms

Search multiple sources instead of assuming one platform has the whole market. Save useful searches and refine them as you learn which titles produce the right opportunities.

🤝

Your network

AI can help you prepare outreach, but the relationship is human. Look for former coworkers, industry contacts, recruiters and people connected to organizations you're genuinely interested in.

🏠

Company career pages

When an opportunity matters, verify the opening and details at the employer itself. It can also reveal related roles that never surfaced in your original search.
