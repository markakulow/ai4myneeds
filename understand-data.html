Understand Data

You don't have to be a data analyst to make data useful.

AI can help you understand what a spreadsheet is showing, learn how to work with it and figure out better questions to ask. The goal isn't to hand your numbers to AI and blindly accept an answer.

⚠️

Important: Use AI as a data guide, not your calculator of record. AI can misread columns, make incorrect calculations or confidently reach the wrong conclusion. Use it to understand structure, learn methods, create formulas and plan analysis. Verify important numbers and conclusions in the source data before using them to make decisions.

Start with the question

What are you trying to understand or build?

You might run a business, manage a team, work in the field, own a shop or simply have a spreadsheet someone expects you to understand. You don't need to know the technical terminology first.

Try it yourself

Use AI to help you work with the data.

These examples focus on learning the path and checking the work, not outsourcing judgment to AI.

📄

Understand a spreadsheet

If someone sends you a spreadsheet full of unfamiliar columns, abbreviations or tabs, AI can act like a patient guide. It can help explain what each field appears to mean, how the information is organized and what questions you may want to ask before you analyze anything. This is especially useful when you inherited a report or were never taught how it works.

Example: “I received this spreadsheet and I'm not sure what I'm supposed to take away from it. First explain the columns and how the information appears to be organized in plain English. Don't analyze the results yet. Tell me anything you don't understand and give me five useful questions I could investigate.”

When your AI tool supports file uploads, you can often attach the spreadsheet directly. Remove sensitive information when appropriate.

Turn a business question into an analysis

You may know the business question but have no idea how to translate it into spreadsheet work. AI can help identify which fields you would need, what should be compared, what information may be missing and what calculation or method could answer the question. You can then build and verify that analysis in the spreadsheet itself.

Example: “I run a small landscaping company. I track customer, job type, quoted price, actual price, labor hours and material cost. I want to understand which types of jobs are most profitable. Don't calculate the answer for me yet. Explain what I should compare, what additional information I may need and how I could build this analysis in a spreadsheet.”

🧮

Learn or build an Excel formula

You do not need to know the name of a formula before asking for help. Describe what you want Excel or Google Sheets to accomplish in everyday language. AI can suggest a formula, explain what each part means, show example cell references and walk you through where to enter it so you learn how the spreadsheet works instead of only copying code.

Example: “I have an employee ID in column A and want Excel to find that employee in another sheet and return their hourly rate. Explain what a lookup formula does, which formula I should consider, and walk me through it step by step using example cell references.”

What's a VLOOKUP? It searches for a value in the first column of a table and returns information from another column in the same row. AI can explain older tools such as VLOOKUP as well as alternatives such as XLOOKUP, and help you understand when each makes sense.

Figure out what to look for

Sometimes the hardest part is knowing what questions to ask. AI can help you create a checklist for trends, unusual changes, outliers, comparisons and missing context. Instead of asking AI to declare what happened, ask it to show you what an experienced manager, owner or analyst would investigate and how you can test those questions yourself.

Example: “This report tracks weekly orders, revenue, cancellations and labor hours. What questions would an experienced operations manager ask before deciding whether performance improved? Explain how I could test each question using the spreadsheet rather than giving me conclusions.”

🛠️

Build a spreadsheet from scratch

If you run a business or manage work using notebooks, emails or scattered files, AI can help you design a simple spreadsheet around what you actually need to track. Describe how the work happens today and what you wish you could see. AI can suggest useful columns, basic formulas, dropdowns or status fields and explain how to build them one step at a time.

Example: “I own a small plumbing business and currently track jobs in a notebook. I want a simple spreadsheet that helps me track customer, service date, job type, invoice amount, payment status and expenses. Explain what columns I should create, what each is for and which simple formulas would be useful. Assume I'm a beginner with Excel.”

Explain the results clearly

Data becomes useful when people understand what it means. After you have checked the numbers yourself, AI can help turn verified findings into plain language for a customer, employee, manager or meeting. It can also help separate what the numbers actually prove from possible explanations that still need investigation.

Example: “I've verified these three findings from our monthly report. Help me explain them to my team in plain language. Separate what the data proves from what might be a possible explanation, and give me questions we should investigate next.”

For managers & business owners

Use data to run the work, not just report on it.

Managers often inherit reports without knowing whether they answer the questions the team actually needs. AI can help you think through what should be measured, define useful KPIs, design scorecards, identify missing context and turn recurring reporting into a clearer operating rhythm.

🎯

Choose useful measures

Start with the decisions you need to make. AI can help translate goals such as improving service, controlling costs or increasing capacity into possible measures and questions to validate with your team.

📋

Build a scorecard

Use AI to think through what belongs on a weekly or monthly view, how metrics relate to each other and what definitions need to be consistent so everyone reads the numbers the same way.

🔍

Ask better questions

Instead of reacting to one red number, use AI to develop follow-up questions about volume, staffing, seasonality, process changes and other context that may explain what you're seeing.

A safer way to use AI with numbers

Let AI teach the method. Let the source data prove the answer.

A useful habit is to ask AI for the steps, formulas, checks and questions behind an analysis. Then perform or verify those steps in Excel, Google Sheets or the system where the data actually lives. This makes AI valuable even when you shouldn't trust it to be the final authority.
