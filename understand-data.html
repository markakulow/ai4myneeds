Understand Data

You don't have to be a data analyst to make data useful.

AI can help you understand what a spreadsheet is showing, learn how to work with it and figure out better questions to ask. The goal isn't to hand your numbers to AI and blindly accept an answer.

⚠️

Use AI as a data guide, not your calculator of record. AI can misread columns, make incorrect calculations or confidently reach the wrong conclusion. Use it to understand structure, learn methods, create formulas and plan analysis. Verify important numbers and conclusions in the source data before using them to make decisions.

Start with the question

What are you trying to understand or build?

You might run a business, manage a team, work in the field, own a shop or simply have a spreadsheet someone expects you to understand. You don't need to know the technical terminology first.

Try it yourself

Use AI to help you work with the data.

These examples focus on learning the path and checking the work, not outsourcing judgment to AI.

📄

Understand a spreadsheet

Start by learning what the file contains before asking AI to draw conclusions from it.

Example: “I received this spreadsheet and I'm not sure what I'm supposed to take away from it. First explain the columns and how the information appears to be organized in plain English. Don't analyze the results yet. Tell me anything you don't understand and give me five useful questions I could investigate.”

When your AI tool supports file uploads, you can often attach the spreadsheet directly. Remove sensitive information when appropriate.

❓

Turn a business question into an analysis

You know what you want to learn, but not which numbers, filters or calculations will get you there.

Example: “I run a small landscaping company. I track customer, job type, quoted price, actual price, labor hours and material cost. I want to understand which types of jobs are most profitable. Don't calculate the answer for me yet. Explain what I should compare, what additional information I may need and how I could build this analysis in a spreadsheet.”

🧮

Learn or build an Excel formula

Describe what you want the spreadsheet to do. AI can explain the formula and walk you through building it.

Example: “I have an employee ID in column A and want Excel to find that employee in another sheet and return their hourly rate. Explain what a lookup formula does, which formula I should consider, and walk me through it step by step using example cell references.”

What's a VLOOKUP? It searches for a value in the first column of a table and returns information from another column in the same row. AI can explain older tools such as VLOOKUP as well as alternatives such as XLOOKUP, and help you understand when each makes sense.

📈

Figure out what to look for

Use AI to suggest checks for trends, changes, outliers and missing context before you start interpreting results.

Example: “This report tracks weekly orders, revenue, cancellations and labor hours. What questions would an experienced operations manager ask before deciding whether performance improved? Explain how I could test each question using the spreadsheet rather than giving me conclusions.”

🛠️

Build a spreadsheet from scratch

If you've never learned spreadsheets, start with the outcome you need instead of the features you don't know.

Example: “I own a small plumbing business and currently track jobs in a notebook. I want a simple spreadsheet that helps me track customer, service date, job type, invoice amount, payment status and expenses. Explain what columns I should create, what each is for and which simple formulas would be useful. Assume I'm a beginner with Excel.”

🗣️

Explain the results clearly

Once you've verified the numbers, AI can help translate the findings for someone who doesn't live in the spreadsheet.

Example: “I've verified these three findings from our monthly report. Help me explain them to my team in plain language. Separate what the data proves from what might be a possible explanation, and give me questions we should investigate next.”

A safer way to use AI with numbers

Let AI teach the method. Let the source data prove the answer.

A useful habit is to ask AI for the steps, formulas, checks and questions behind an analysis. Then perform or verify those steps in Excel, Google Sheets or the system where the data actually lives. This makes AI valuable even when you shouldn't trust it to be the final authority.
